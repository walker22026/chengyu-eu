AI isn't just a lab concept anymore — it's genuinely part of daily life now. Today, right as I got my hands on GPT-5, I also started experimenting with using AI to help with some of my own regular investing research.

### What can it actually do?

ChatGPT's range is broad — it helps out with both work and everyday life:

- **Technical assistant** — helping me work through programming problems, network configuration, data analysis, even writing solid code.
- **Language tutor** — when I'm learning a foreign language, it breaks down grammar, explains vocabulary, corrects pronunciation, and generates practice exercises.
- **Creative partner** — whether it's a blog post, a video script, or putting together a slide deck, it can quickly produce something well-structured and coherent.
- **Research assistant** — when I'm looking into ETFs or retirement planning, it helps me pull information, analyze data, and compare options.
- **Everyday helper** — travel planning, food suggestions, study plans, day-to-day questions — I turn to it for all of it.

![A GPT-5 session from the first day](https://media.chengyu.eu/images/2025/08/08/2025-08-08-16.14.40.jpg)

### What stands out about it

If I had to sum up ChatGPT in a few words: it's an **omnivore for information** — it can talk about almost anything, from quantum physics to latte art. It's **structure-obsessed**, always able to break something complicated down into a clear framework. It's **detail-oriented** — the more context I give it, the better it tailors what it gives back. And its **tone is adjustable** — it can be serious and professional, or relaxed and funny, depending on what I need.

In my day-to-day work and life, ChatGPT has become something I genuinely rely on. From handling fiddly technical details to turning scattered thoughts into a finished piece of writing, it consistently saves me real time and effort.

### Where this could go

AI is really just getting started. I expect tools like ChatGPT to keep getting smarter and closer to how people actually think, taking on more creative work and eventually helping with problems we can't even picture yet. For me, it's stopped being just a piece of software — it's more like a friend working alongside me in the digital world.
